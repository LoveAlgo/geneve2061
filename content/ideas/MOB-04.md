vision: >-
  In 2061, roadworks in Geneva are rare and short. Under the main streets, utility tunnels carry water,
  gas, electricity, heating and data lines, so they can be inspected and repaired without digging up the
  road, as under rue du Marché since the 1980s. When surface works are needed, they are planned years
  ahead, coordinated between all the networks, and carried out quickly, often over a weekend or a few
  nights, with prefabricated elements and silent machines.

today_detail: >-
  Geneva’s main shopping streets, including rue du Marché, got their utility tunnel during their last
  major renovation in the 1980s, but their roadway is now badly damaged. Heavy maintenance would take
  12 to 18 months and cost 26 million francs; a full redesign, 24 to 32 months and 40 to 50 million. A
  municipal motion asking for the renovation was postponed eleven times, and accepted five years after
  it was filed.

view: |-
  Roadworks in Geneva often seem to last forever. Elsewhere, things are done much faster: the Dutch replace whole motorway bridges over a weekend, and their bridges do not fall apart. So what prevents us from doing things efficiently in Switzerland? I think several factors come into play: corruption, which always slows things down and makes them more expensive; some laws, such as limits on working hours; poor planning and incompetence; and perhaps the wish for everything to be perfect.

  Utility tunnels like the one under rue du Marché show that a good solution has existed in Geneva for decades. It just needs to become the norm.
