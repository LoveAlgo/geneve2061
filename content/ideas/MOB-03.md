today_headline: Geneva is studying an automatic underground railway, under the
  lake, from the Pays de Gex to Saint-Julien
today_detail: Presented by the Council of State in November 2025, the
  Jura–Léman–Salève line would serve CERN, the airport, Meyrin, the
  international organisations, the city centre, Carouge and two connections in
  France. Designed for 160,000 passengers a day with a train every three
  minutes, it could carry up to 400,000, and would bring half the canton’s
  population and 70% of its jobs within 750 metres of a station.
today_source: Council of State of Geneva, Liaison Jura – Léman – Salève, press

today_facts:
  - The Grand Council has voted a credit of 30 million francs for the
    preliminary studies, and a popular vote is foreseen. The project was not
    prioritised in the federal expert report “Transports 2045”, because no
    preliminary studies were available at the time (same source).
  - Among the main challenges identified are groundwater, excavated material,
    the crossing of the lake, and the dense network of existing underground
    pipes and cables in the city centre (same source).

questions:
  - How can an underground network grow without overcrowding the city centre,
    for example by turning some city-centre offices into homes and building new
    offices in the outskirts?
  - Why does it take as long today to build a few dozen kilometres of railway in
    a city as it took to build thousands of kilometres across Canada in the
    1880s, and how can that change?
  - How can the tunnels avoid CERN’s accelerator tunnels, the groundwater and
    the existing underground networks of the city centre?
view: >-
  With about a million inhabitants in Greater Geneva, an underground is a must,
  and the success of the Léman Express shows this is the right path forward. It
  would replace much of what runs above ground today, from trams and buses to
  cars, and allow people living even further away in neighbouring France to
  reach the city centre easily.


  Switzerland has world-class tunnelling know-how. If we remove corruption and
  incompetence, and use the help of AI, the costs need not go through the roof,
  as they do for most public works.


  But the aim is not to overcrowd the city centre. If some of today’s offices
  were turned into apartments, and new offices built in the outskirts, closer to
  where people live, fewer people would need to commute far.
