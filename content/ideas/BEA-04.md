view: |-
  In Hans Christian Andersen’s fable The Emperor’s New Clothes, it takes an innocent child to say that the emperor is naked. The same has happened in architecture over the last century: people accepted soulless buildings because the weavers convinced them they were beautiful. Fortunately, more and more people now see that the emperor has no clothes.

  Architecture is public art. It affects everyone who walks past, so architects have a huge responsibility. Vitruvius’s principles of strength, function and beauty, which combine masculine and feminine energy, seem to have been forgotten. And architectural beauty existed long before Vitruvius: think of the Egyptians.

  Good architecture is not a question of taste. If it were, millions of people would queue to see the Montparnasse Tower instead of the Eiffel Tower. Buildings should also use natural, local materials, like the beautiful sandstone buildings of Adelaide, so that they fit into their landscape. Ugly buildings often last less long, cost more to maintain and create bad vibes around them.
