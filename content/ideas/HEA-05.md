vision: Most babies in Geneva are born in calm, homely birth centres run by
  midwives in every neighbourhood, or at home for families who choose it. Birth
  is treated as a natural event rather than a medical procedure, at its own pace
  and on its own day of the week. Skilled emergency care is always within a few
  minutes for the rare births that need it.
today_headline: Only about 2 in 100 births in Switzerland take place in a birth centre
today_detail: In 2023, 1,600 of the 79,200 births in Swiss hospitals and birth
  centres took place in a birth centre. In Geneva, labour was artificially
  induced in half of the births not planned as caesareans, the highest rate in
  the country. In other words, fewer than half of all babies in Geneva are born
  after a labour that started on its own.
today_source: Federal Statistical Office (FSO), press release on births in 2023 (12 May 2025)
today_facts:
  - One birth in three in Switzerland is by caesarean section (34% in 2023),
    which places the country among those with the most caesareans in Europe.
    Geneva’s rate is lower, at 29%, down 4.2 points in ten years (same source).
  - Half of all births now take place under epidural anaesthesia, against 38% in
    2014 (same source).
  - Hospitals record about 30% more births on weekdays than at weekends, because
    most planned caesareans and inductions are scheduled during the week. In
    birth centres, births are spread evenly over the week (same source).
precedents:
  - place: England
    text: studied almost 65,000 low-risk births in the Birthplace study. Births
      planned in midwife-led units were as safe for the baby as births planned
      in hospital obstetric units, with substantially fewer interventions and
      caesareans. For second and later babies, planned home births were equally
      safe. For first babies, a planned home birth carried a higher risk for the
      baby (9.3 against 5.3 adverse outcomes per 1,000 births), so birth centres
      are the safer choice for first-time mothers.

questions:
  - How can births outside hospital be made as safe as possible for first
    babies, where planned home births carry a higher risk?
  - Why is labour induced so much more often in Geneva than elsewhere in
    Switzerland?
  - How many more midwives will Geneva need, and how will they be trained?
view: "Giving birth is not an illness. Most healthy pregnancies need patience,
  warmth and a skilled midwife, not an operating theatre. When birth is
  organised around hospital schedules, we create a demand for interventions that
  many women do not need. The hospital should be close by for those who need it,
  not the default for everyone. And let nature finish its work: when the cord is
  left to pulse for a few minutes before it is cut, the baby receives the blood
  still in the placenta, with iron for its first months of life."
