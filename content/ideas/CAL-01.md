today_detail: >-
  Geneva is the Swiss canton with the highest share of its population exposed to excessive road traffic
  noise, and the third in absolute numbers, after Vaud and Zurich. In 2022, 18% of the 51,736 building
  façades assessed were still above the legal limits, down from 24% five years earlier.

today_facts:
- >-
  Geneva has been a pioneer of noise-absorbing road surfaces since 1998, and by the end of 2022 about
  98% of the cantonal roads needing noise treatment had one. Yet the volume of traffic still keeps noise
  above the limits in many streets (Council of State, 2022).
- >-
  Since 2022, the canton has been lowering speed limits to reduce noise: 30 km/h day and night in the
  city centre, and 30 km/h at night on many main roads. Going from 50 to 30 km/h reduces noise by about
  3 decibels, the same effect as halving the traffic (Department of Infrastructure, 2022).
- >-
  The World Health Organization recommends keeping road traffic noise below 53 decibels on average and
  45 at night. Above these levels, noise is linked to heart disease, sleep disturbance and annoyance (WHO,
  2018).

view: |-
  Being hypersensitive to noise myself, I have thought a lot about how to create quieter communities. Noise causes stress and long-term health problems, whether we consciously notice it or not, and it is one of the main reasons for quarrels between neighbours.

  The solution to most city noise can be summed up in one word: walkability. Because the decibel scale is logarithmic, halving the number of cars is not enough to halve the noise: traffic must fall by something like 80 to 90%. Noise-absorbing asphalt is the best traffic-noise measure I have seen, yet it has still not become mainstream, simply because people are so used to noise that they do not ask for it.

  When people stopped making noise during the pandemic, wildlife returned to city centres. Imagine what a quiet Geneva could give back to us.
