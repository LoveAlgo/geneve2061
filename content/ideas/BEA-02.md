today_headline: Geneva once had its own covered galleries, the “dômes” of the

today_detail: From the late 18th century, wooden canopies and arcades known as
  “dômes” lined the Rues-Basses and sheltered pedestrians from the weather. One
  Geneva architect calls them Genevan archetypes. During the Restoration that
  followed 1814, the city stripped the street of them. Today, the principles
  that made such streets beautiful are taught in every art school, yet they
  rarely guide what is built.

today_facts:
  - The elements of visual art (line, shape, form, space, colour, value and
    texture) and its principles (balance, proportion, symmetry, contrast,
    emphasis, repetition, rhythm and unity) are the basic grammar of art and
    design courses.
  - Eye-tracking studies in Boston, New York and nearby towns found that people
    barely look at blank façades or walls of repetitive glass, while buildings
    with punched windows and symmetrical areas of high contrast consistently
    catch the eye (Sussman and Hollander, since 2015).
  - When a blank wall in Cincinnati received a colourful mural, eye-tracking
    software predicted that 88 to 96% of first glances would fall on the centre
    of the mural, the area that was ignored when the wall was blank (The Human
    Architecture and Planning Institute).

questions:
  - How much symmetry is right? Too little feels chaotic and too much feels
    rigid, and slopes, views and nature must be respected. Can the right balance
    be described in simple rules?
  - How can a street keep its unity while every owner keeps the freedom to
    express their own taste?
  - Can the seasonal colour analysis used in fashion help choose the colours of
    a neighbourhood, with warm or cool palettes that suit its stone, light and
    landscape?
view: >-
  Everything we do should be a work of art: our clothes, our houses, our dishes,
  our gardens, our parks. Good art is both functional and aesthetic, and the aim
  is elegance, a state where you cannot add or take away anything without making
  it worse. Ornament creates a binding energy that makes something feel whole.


  The Elements and Principles of Visual Arts are taught in most art schools. So
  why do we see so few real-life applications of them? Most modern art and
  architecture simply do not follow these principles, which is why they feel
  wrong and ugly to so many people.


  Symmetry matters both in buildings and in town planning, although we have to
  take nature and topography into account, and too much symmetry does not look
  good either. It is an art. The same goes for colour: colour theory was
  developed mostly for beauty and fashion, but its principles apply just as well
  to interiors, painting and the whole city.
