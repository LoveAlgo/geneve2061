  In 2061, every major building and public space in Geneva is tested before it is built. Volunteers walk
  through it in virtual reality wearing eye-trackers and heart-rate monitors, and the results are published.

  a world centre for the science of beauty and well-being in cities.

view: |-
  Some people still argue that art and architecture are a question of taste. This myth is now being scientifically disproven with eye-tracking tests of people walking in cities: their eyes subconsciously avoid ugly architecture, as the research of Dr Ann Sussman shows.

  If good architecture were a question of taste, millions of people would queue to see the Montparnasse Tower instead of the Eiffel Tower. In the future, Geneva needs to take science-based, and common-sense-based, decisions about what is built.

  Poor-quality, dysfunctional and ugly architecture makes our subconscious work in the background, which generates stress, and stress leads to sickness. This means that the key to improving human health may lie in a better design of our built environment.
